## About Me

I am a PhD Candidate in Statistics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst. My research interests span statistical methodology, nonparametric regression, computational statistics, likelihood-based inference, quantitative modeling, and statistical learning.

Beyond methodological research, I am interested in applying statistical and computational methods to problems in finance and financial markets, economics, population health, insurance, urban mobility, and other data-rich settings.

My work is motivated by a simple question: **what can the data tell us, what can it not tell us, and how do our statistical choices shape the answer?**

## Current Research

My doctoral research at UMass Amherst focuses primarily on statistical methodology, including:

- Global bandwidth selection for local polynomial derivative estimation
- Nonparametric regression and smoothing
- Likelihood theory for binary regression
- Statistical computing and simulation

I also work on applied research involving health, economics, finance, insurance, and other quantitative problems.

## Selected Projects

The site highlights selected projects in areas including:
